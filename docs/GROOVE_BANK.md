# Rhythmic pad, acid, house and organ additions

These independently programmed patches follow the owner's recipes with original names. They do not embed the referenced songs, MIDI or third-party samples.

- **Nebula Pulse Pad (F129):** five-voice three-frame saw/rounded-saw/octave-saw wavetable, a three-voice soft-brass colour layer one octave up about 8 dB below A, and quiet noise for air. Full sustain, 0.84 s attack and 2.16 s release. Four-bar wavetable scanning, two-bar cutoff movement and eight-bar width movement. Smooth full-range gate with 12% swing; Macro 1/Energy controls gate depth from continuous pad to chopped rhythm. Chorus, reverb and high-pass EQ add space. No separate granular oscillator layer is claimed.
- **Caustic Circuit Bass (F130):** single saw, legato glide with 65 ms time constant, 300 Hz 24 dB low-pass, 70% resonance and a 220 ms cutoff-envelope decay with zero sustain. Drive adds grit. Velocity opens cutoff and raises level, including when a new legato note changes velocity. A quieter short delay adds width.
- **Velvet Transit Chords (F131):** sine/triangle FM, an upward arpeggio with an eight-step velocity pattern, gentle internal pumping, dotted-eighth ping-pong delay, plate-like reverb and a broad 3.5 kHz notch. Hold a chord: this articulates its notes rather than firing the whole chord on every step. The pattern uses existing ARP + SEQ mode, without copied song notes. Per-step pitch offsets remain zero. The cutoff envelope is 0.8 s and the slow LFO is four bars; the recipe's incompatible slow-envelope and rapid-stab suggestions are balanced for audible short steps.
- **Drawbar Ember Bass (F132):** sine at the played pitch, a quieter octave-up sine and a centred sub sine one octave below. Full sustain and short release. Velocity-to-volume is disabled; velocity instead changes the filter tone. A small filter transient and very light chorus keep it percussive and warm. This follows the sine organ route rather than the conflicting saw-bass alternative in the recipe.

## Synth additions

**FX → Trance gate → Swing** delays alternate gate-step boundaries while preserving the two-step duration. Zero preserves straight timing; attack/release smooth the edges. Macro destinations now append **Wavetable Scan** and **Gate Depth**, preserving previous destination indices. Set these through ENGINES' modulation routing controls. Scan movement applies to the custom wavetable engine. Gate depth modulation is global at audio-block rate.

**ENGINES → FILTER ENV → Velocity to cutoff** specifies the cutoff span in octaves across the velocity range, centred on velocity 0.5. Defaults to zero. **ENGINES → ANALOGUE → Velocity to volume** defaults to 100%, preserving the previous response; zero makes amp level independent of velocity. The new **Sub sine level** defaults to zero and adds a centred sine one octave below oscillator pitch, bypassing the voice filter but retaining the amp envelope and shared output/effects chain. These additions act on synth voices; sub is not added to sample voices.

The Nebula wavetable is generated locally and embedded in saved states. Selecting this factory patch replaces the active custom table with its own three frames; saved user patches retain their embedded tables. The native groove test checks swung step timing, velocity accents separately from amplitude, flat organ dynamics, sub filter bypass, real wavetable modulation, long pad level, accented arpeggiator MIDI/audio and state recall.

- **Copper Bounce Bass (F133):** octave-down saw plus a sine sub at the same low octave, 24 dB low-pass, short independent filter/amp envelopes, velocity-driven cutoff accents and a small 45 ms pitch bump that scales with velocity. Moderate saturation and quiet 75 ms slapback add punch. ENGINES → ANALOGUE now includes velocity-to-pitch-envelope depth; its zero default retains the original fixed pitch-envelope amount. Play bass notes in a suitable octave: transposing both the played note and oscillator down can place the fundamental below useful range.

The expanded destination menu keeps raw saved destination indices, but existing host automation of the destination-selector choices may need checking because their normalized range now contains two additional entries.
